# The world

**Core idea, one sentence:** you don't get to argue your side until you've argued theirs well
enough that *they* sign off on it.

**Obvious version (not building it):** a soft pastel "communication coach" app with a cartoon
wombat mascot, a hero line like "Fight Better Together", three feature cards and a footer.

## Three pitches

1. **Border control.** Every person is a country. To enter your partner's country you apply
   for a visa by stating their case in their own terms, and only *they* can stamp it ADMITTED
   or DENIED. The Wombat is the customs officer with an X-ray machine for your luggage.
2. **Hostage negotiation.** The Wombat is a negotiator on a crackly line, and you're both
   holding the relationship hostage. You repeat the other side's demands until they say
   "that's right".
3. **Ventriloquist night.** You have to perform your partner as a puppet on stage. The
   audience (your partner) decides whether the impression is fair or a cheap shot.

## Picked: border control

Because the method *is* a visa. A steelman is a request to enter someone else's point of view,
and the one rule that makes steelmanning work (only the other person can say you got it right)
is exactly how a border works: you don't stamp your own passport. Every screen has a reason
to exist inside it:

- the private version is a **customs declaration** (with a "do you have anything to declare"
  checklist of grievances);
- the Wombat's translation is **what customs actually found in the bag**;
- the BS Meter is the **X-ray**: it scans your steelman for smuggled complaints;
- the free phrase check is the **sniffer dog**;
- handing the phone over is **"Next traveller, step forward"**;
- the verdict is the **inspection report**, the solutions are **treaty proposals**, the final
  agreement is a **treaty** both sign, and the 7-day check-in is **treaty review**;
- Emergency Wombat is the **diplomatic incident hotline**;
- loading is "your bag is in the X-ray"; an outage is "customs closed"; a crash is
  "lost luggage".

The embarrassing part: the declaration checklist ("Your mother's opinion", "Receipts from
2019", "I'm fine (lie)") and a passport that prints DENIED in red ink across your attempt
at empathy.

Fonts: Cormorant SC (engraved government serif) and Courier Prime (typewriter, forms).
The machine-readable zone at the foot of every page carries the travellers' real names.
